## Problem

Measurement artifacts in trustworthiness scores of quantized sub-2B language models: score changes attributed to quantization can come from the measuring instrument changing with precision.

## Technique

Audit the instrument while holding models and items fixed. Vary one measurement choice at a time; use paired exact McNemar tests and flip counts; calibrate detectors against adjudicated human labels with a two-phase sampling estimator; compare scoring rules against a split-half reliability ceiling; permute batch order and padding to measure instrument-induced flips; recenter the answer logit before reading ECE; save every option's raw score so rules can be rescored offline.

## Foundation

Δmeas = Δtrue + Δinst. For a binary outcome m_p = s_p*π_p + (1-c_p)*(1-π_p): constant sensitivity/specificity can only rescale a paired change, but precision-dependent error (s_q-s_f, c_q-c_f) can create or reverse the sign. Split-half reliability bounds the correlation two protocols can reach. Rounding activations to 8 bits turns small batch-shape and padding differences into different argmax choices.

## Compute

PyTorch 2.8, CUDA 12.8, Transformers 4.57, bitsandbytes 0.50. Baseline on NVIDIA A10G; protocol, batch, padding and regeneration runs on a 6 GB laptop GPU; mechanism test also on a cloud T4. Authors report roughly one day for the 24-cell protocol grid plus the batch test, 23,280 responses judged by WildGuard 7B, and 100 human annotations. A DRock-side audit needs only repeat benchmark runs; reproducing the int8/NF4 numbers needs CUDA, which this Arc A380 machine does not have.

## Reported

8 instruction-tuned models, 135M-1.7B, at float16, LLM.int8() and NF4, paired on identical items. Keyword refusal detector recall 33.3% (95% CI 21.4-47.9) and precision 68.2% (47.3-83.6) vs human labels; corrected detector 80%/80%; WildGuard 95.2%/83.3% for refusal. 4-bit refusal: -4.7 points by corrected keyword, -3.6 (CI -4.4 to -2.8) human-calibrated, with harmful compliance +2.6 (1.7-3.5), +3.0 using WildGuard cells. SmolLM2-1.7B on 2,000 HaluEval items: +9.8 mean-cloze, +0.8 summed-cloze, -13.6 lettered released order, -7.3 lettered two-order average. Cloze vs lettered effect estimates correlate at rho=0.10 against a noise ceiling of ~0.77 (CI 0.69-0.83); split-half reliability 0.70 cloze, 0.84 lettered. NF4 changes first-position rate in 8/8 models, up to 11.0 points pooled, up to 59.5 on ARC-Easy for one model. Batch rearrangement changes 0.8-6.4% of 8-bit choices (float16 at most 0.6%), equal to 57-119% of the float16-to-8-bit flip rate; padding changes 2.2-5.6% (float16 at most 0.4%). Effect survives with outlier decomposition off (up to 5.8% batch, 7.4% padding) and disappears with float16 activations; NF4 at most 0.4%/0.2%. Calibration: SmolLM2-135M BoolQ ECE +0.154 (CI 0.130-0.173) becomes -0.031 after recentring (Yes rate 0.58 to 0.19, class AUROC 0.517/0.553); Llama-3.2-1B +0.037 becomes -0.008. 11/80 ECE intervals exclude zero vs ~4 by chance; 48/80 equivalent to zero at 0.02 margin, 68/80 at 0.03; mean gap between two executions of one configuration 0.0035.

## Reproducibility

Paper claims release of item-level predictions, raw option scores, detector outputs and human annotations, but the arXiv abstract page and full text contain no repository, DOI or download URL; a web search found none. Reported settings are detailed: HF checkpoint revisions for all 8 models, WildGuard revision cbba482, 30 keyword markers quoted, 64-token greedy generations in left-padded batches of 8, 5000 bootstrap resamples, 1000 split-half splits, 3 identical 4-bit runs for determinism, fixed seed for the 1000-item ARC sample (seed value not given), shuffle seeds not given. Two annotators, Cohen kappa 0.380 before adjudication.

## Drock application

benchmark/paired.py:397 decide() treats PASS/FAIL flips between two BEFORE runs as the noise a gain must beat; that floor is valid only if the arm under test leaves the checker and prompt path unchanged. The same applies to false-success flags at benchmark/paired.py:429 and to the stage labels in benchmark/contracts.py:21. activity.py:539 UNSAFE and act_bulk.py:150 _clear_refusal are regex-based gates of the kind the paper shows can miss real instances.

## Modifications

In benchmark/paired.py decide(), add an instrument-flip count from two identical runs with permuted task order or batching and require gain > max(BEFORE flips, instrument flips); report per-task flips beside pass totals. In benchmark/check.py, store the raw per-requirement signals, not just PASS/FAIL, so a second rule can be scored offline. For any regex gate (activity.py UNSAFE, _clear_refusal), add a small human-labelled set and report recall/precision at each arm, as the paper does for refusal detectors. Before claiming two checkers disagree, compute split-half reliability on the same tasks and read agreement against that ceiling.

## Limitations

Paper covers 8 models and only bitsandbytes int8/NF4; GPTQ, AWQ and GGUF untested. Human sample is 100 items with fair pre-adjudication agreement, and the crucial assumption that within-cell human rates do not change with precision is untested: a 2.6-point shift either way erases or doubles the harmful-compliance estimate; the 51 annotated f16/4-bit responses give 4.9 points with CI -23.1 to 31.0. Safety is English AdvBench with 64-token greedy output; hallucination is preference among given answers, not free generation. Baseline and protocol runs used different hardware, so some effects differ by tenths of a point. No released artifact URL means the item-level data cannot currently be checked.
