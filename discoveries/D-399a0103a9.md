## Problem

On RISC-V Vector (RVV) cores, LLM inference state that lives across many instructions is hard to express in per-instruction RVV semantics. Two cases: native GGML block-quantized weights (scales, sub-blocks, integer dot products, ordered FP32 accumulation) and the K/V cache during decode attention. Plain RVV kernels pay heavily in instruction count and data movement for both.

## Technique

Two hardware extensions added beside RVV, scoped by how long the data state lives. (1) Quantized Block Streams (QBS) is one execution process that decodes native block formats, walks the blocks, does the integer math and accumulates in FP32 in a fixed order. It works on an activation snapshot that is already quantized to Q8_K, and that snapshot can be reused across projections. (2) Attention/KV Context (AKV) keeps K/V resident in a local store and gives two operand views aligned with the compute direction: K for QK^T and V for PV. Softmax and the rest of the attention arithmetic stay on RVV. Softmax state across tiles, scheduling, and F32-to-Q8_K activation quantization stay in software.

## Foundation

Computer architecture, not new math. It is a microarchitecture and ISA extension to the Ara RVV vector processor, using llama.cpp/GGML block-quantized dot products (K-quant super-blocks with per-sub-block scales) and the usual decode-attention dataflow (QK^T, softmax, PV) with GQA/MQA K/V sharing.

## Compute

Custom RTL. Baseline: Ara, 4 lanes, VLEN=1024, ELEN=64, DLEN=256, 128-bit AXI, TSMC 28 nm HPC+, 1 GHz, cycle-accurate RTL simulation (simulator not named). Using it requires taping out or FPGA-prototyping a modified RISC-V vector core. It does not run on x86 CPUs or Intel GPUs.

## Reported

All numbers come from the authors' cycle-accurate RTL simulation at TSMC 28 nm, 1 GHz, against an Ara RVV baseline that already has optimized multi-output RVV kernels. Projection at M=1, N=256, K=1536 (Q3_K-Q6_K) or K=896 (Q8_0), speedup over RVV for Compute / Matmul: Q3_K 44.2x / 72.7x, Q5_K 60.6x / 92.5x, Q6_K 35.0x / 50.7x, Q8_0 17.3x / 20.4x. Geometric means 35.68x / 51.34x. Decode attention (D128, GQA6, 2 K/V heads) vs Tiled RVV: 1.45x geometric mean, from 1.22x at KV16 to 1.60x at KV256. Refact-1.6B shape (D64, MQA32) 2.04x and Gemma 3 shape (D256, GQA4) 2.55x vs Direct RVV. Reusing one activation snapshot across three Q4_K projections cuts cycles by 47.6%. Two-query AKV sharing at M=15 prefill cuts cycles by 14.5%. Area 3.195 to 3.599 mm2 (+12.65%). Projection throughput 0.615 to 32.29 GOPS. Throughput per area 0.192 to 8.97 GOPS/mm2 (46.63x). Power 177.0 to 471.7 mW (+166.49%). Energy 312.8 to 16.91 pJ/op, i.e. 3.55 to 70.18 GOPS/W (19.78x). Projection vector instruction count is 0.42-0.56% of baseline. ## Reproducibility

No code, no RTL, no artifact statement. The only link is arXiv's HTML build log. Workloads are synthetic layer shapes; Qwen2.5 and Qwen3 are named, but model sizes are not given. My guess is that K=896 and K=1536 are the hidden sizes of Qwen2.5-0.5B and 1.5B; the paper does not say so. Seeds do not apply (deterministic RTL sim). The simulator and cache configuration are not stated. Nobody could reproduce this without the authors' RTL.

## Drock application

none. DRock runs Qwen GGUF models through Ollama on an i5-8400 (x86) and an Intel Arc A380. Neither is a RISC-V vector core, and QBS/AKV are silicon changes, not software. The only overlap is that the paper evaluates the same GGML formats (Q3_K-Q8_0, Q8_K activations) that DRock's Ollama models use. That is shared vocabulary, not something DRock can call.

## Modifications

Nothing to port directly. The nearest software idea is my opinion, not something the paper tests. AKV's point is to keep K/V resident and stop rebuilding it, and in DRock the matching lever is Ollama's prompt/KV cache: keep the system prompt and tool grammar as a byte-identical prefix across calls so llama.cpp reuses the cached K/V instead of re-prefilling. That can already be measured with latency.jsonl and prompt_budget.py and needs nothing from this paper. Activation-snapshot reuse across Q/K/V projections is a kernel concern inside llama.cpp, out of DRock's reach.

## Limitations

Stated or implied by the authors: split-K results are not bitwise identical to unsplit runs; activation snapshots and AKV contexts do not track writes to source memory, so software must rebuild or release them; F32-to-Q8_K quantization, softmax state across tiles and segment scheduling stay in software; hard interface limits on M/N ranges, K-block count, head dimension and token count; no dedicated limitations section. My opinion: the baseline is slow (0.615 GOPS), which makes the 35-51x look large; most of the gain is removing per-element dequant instructions that any dedicated unit would remove. Power rises 2.67x. Prompt-length attention results top out at KV256, far short of real decode contexts. MHA and MLA are not evaluated. There is no end-to-end tokens/s for a whole model, only per-layer kernels.
