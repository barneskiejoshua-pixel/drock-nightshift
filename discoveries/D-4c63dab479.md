## Problem

4-bit weight, activation, and KV-cache quantization of a world action model keeps the generated action from matching the 16-bit policy, because the remaining action error comes through a predicted future latent that action-only quantizers do not protect.

## Technique

LAQ, post-training and with no extra training. A reader metric prices an error in the future latent by how much it changes the action. A read count weights each rounding error by how many denoising steps reuse that same error. One threshold then chooses which activation tokens and cache entries get a second 4-bit residual code (gaze-guided refinement). Gaze at run time is the attention from the action tokens. Every stored weight stays 4-bit. GPTQ in randomized Hadamard coordinates, per-token activation scales, one KV scale per token and head.

## Foundation

Gauss-Newton metric of the reader: M = E[J^T D J], with J the Jacobian of the action reader w.r.t. the future latent and D the diagonal curvature of a soft-histogram KL on the action. Read count n_u is the ratio of frozen-error damage (1^T Omega 1) to fresh-error damage (tr Omega), estimated by injecting equal-energy errors rather than differentiating the sampler. The joint objective separates to second order, and a budget on a second 4-bit pass (rounding-error power down by at least 196) is a single threshold on n_u * omega_u * sigma_u^2 / c_u. Builds on GPTQ second-order rounding and Hadamard rotation. Not quantization-aware training.

## Compute

Offline, on the full-precision model: Rademacher reverse passes through the reader to estimate M, plus a few dozen forward interventions for read counts, then GPTQ. The paper's calibration figure is 1.2 GPU-hours and 27 GB peak, described as forward passes of the quantized model on 80 LIBERO contexts. Inference is W4A4KV4 with a second 4-bit GEMM on the selected rows only. On Fast-WAM that budget refines 15.4% of denoising-loop activation values and 6.0% of cached keys and values. Measured on an NVIDIA A100 using integer tensor cores: one policy call 888 ms, peak policy memory 6.0 GB. No Jetson Orin NX number is reported, though that board is the stated motive (BF16 Fast-WAM weights 12 GB of a 16 GB board).

## Modifications

None that stay inside DRock. LAQ assumes a denoising world-action model whose future latent is re-read by a separate action reader, plus Hadamard-GPTQ and packed 4-bit integer GEMMs. DRock's path is a small Qwen model in Ollama, a deterministic executor, and a verification gate. There is no future latent, no denoising loop, and no KV cache that this allocation could score.

## Limitations

Reported by the setup, not as a limitations section: WAM-specific quantizers were not compared; every baseline keeps BF16 caches while LAQ does not; 0.01% of weights and the timestep tables stay full precision; the real-robot run re-estimates the reader metric after fine-tuning, so the 'no per-task recalibration' claim is about a new task on a fixed model, not a new fine-tune; speed is an A100 tensor-core number, not the Jetson Orin NX named in the introduction. The paper does not report seeds, variance, or the simulated episode count. My reading: the 1.2-point average hides suite losses, especially Goal (Fast-WAM 64.3 vs 67.6 BF16 and vs 68.2 SVDQuant; Cosmos 68.6 vs 73.3 BF16). The real-robot 47 vs 52 average counts a both-objects column that BF16 itself passes only 2/25, while shelf and stacking stay within two trials of BF16 and QuaRot falls to 17%. The 3.8x and 1.66x figures need integer tensor cores and a 6 GB policy footprint that already fills the Arc A380, before any non-CUDA kernel gap. Calibration at 27 GB does not fit that GPU.
