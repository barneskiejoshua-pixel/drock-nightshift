## Problem

Latent chain-of-thought VLMs, which use continuous thoughts in place of text rationales, still run at full precision. Direct 4-bit weight and activation quantization breaks their reasoning accuracy.

## Technique

QL-VLM has two parts. DGQ (Dynamics-Guided Quantization) uses nonuniform codebooks that keep rotary-key (RoPE) structure intact in attention, so vision-language retrieval distorts less. DPD (Dynamics-Preserving Distillation) is quantization-aware training. It aligns the student's continuous thoughts, latent-query readouts and predictions with a frozen full-precision teacher. The starting point is CODI latent CoT adapted to a VLM, followed by one QAT epoch per dataset.

## Foundation

Quantization-aware training with nonuniform codebooks on linear weights and activations and on attention Q/K/V and scores. The codebooks are shaped to keep rotary positional structure. Knowledge distillation from an FP teacher with losses on hidden states and outputs. Operator-level sensitivity and perturbation analysis, which finds that errors in continuous thoughts affect predictions differently depending on their direction.

## Compute

Training: a full-precision CODI latent-CoT checkpoint per dataset, a frozen FP teacher and a QAT pass with gradients, which needs a datacenter-class GPU. Training cost is not reported. Inference latency was measured on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 with custom compiled CUDA low-bit GEMM kernels, falling back to BF16 GEMM for unsupported ops.

## Reported

Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct on ScienceQA-IMG: 4-bit QL-VLM gets 90.43% accuracy. The baseline is 50.10%, a 4-bit Cartesian QAT baseline with the same other quantizers and adapted CODI supervision. The abstract calls the 40.33-point gap '40.33%'. Against explicit CoT it reports 16.64x fewer bit-operations and 6.40x lower measured latency, on the RTX PRO 6000. Bit widths are 4 and 8 for weights, activations and attention in both backbones. The output head and reasoning projection stay in floating point. A-OKVQA and M3CoT are also evaluated, but I did not get their numbers. All main-table results are single runs.

## Reproducibility

The arXiv page has no code link and the HTML text has no code-release statement. The datasets are public (ScienceQA-IMG, A-OKVQA, M3CoT), but distillation data details are deferred to an appendix I did not see. No seeds and no variance across training runs. The only confidence intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals over 8 held-out examples in the sensitivity figure. The CUDA kernels are not released.

## Drock application

none. DRock runs stock Qwen text models through Ollama on an Arc A380. It does not train, does not use latent CoT and has no VLM. The method needs QAT with a teacher, custom CUDA low-bit kernels and a CODI-style latent-reasoning model. None of those runs on an Intel Arc A380 under Ollama/llama.cpp, and DRock's model weights are prebuilt GGUF quants. One general point does carry over: the paper says quantization error in reasoning paths costs far more accuracy than reconstruction error suggests. That supports checking DRock's Q4 against a higher-precision quant on the false-success corpus rather than trusting perplexity, but that is DRock's existing benchmark practice, not this technique.

## Modifications

To use it at all, DRock would need to train a latent-CoT text model, write SYCL/Vulkan low-bit kernels in place of CUDA and stop emitting text actions, because the executor and gate parse explicit action text. Continuous thoughts also cannot be read by the verification gate or replayed from traces. That defeats DRock's audit design.

## Limitations

No code. Single runs with no seed variance. The 40-point gain is against the authors' own 4-bit QAT baseline, not against established PTQ methods such as GPTQ or AWQ, as far as I read. Latency is measured on one high-end NVIDIA GPU with custom kernels. The speedup compares against explicit CoT, so it mixes the gain from fewer steps with the gain from lower precision. Results are on multiple-choice VQA, not agentic tool use. Training cost is not reported.
