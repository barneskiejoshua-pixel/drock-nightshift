## Problem

KV-cache memory and bandwidth growth in long-context reasoning LLM inference. DRock has no open problem about KV cache; the nearest is P-1c7f3895b6 (prompt budget ceiling), but that is prompt text size, not the model's internal KV cache.

## Technique

Gossamer: a constant-budget KV cache manager that picks evict, merge, or quantize per token with a Dynamic Action-Utility argmax. It pads the decode-attention left operand with r=2 auxiliary rows (new normalized key plus running key accumulator) so cosine similarity is computed inside the attention GEMM in idle cycles, called Free-Lunch Utilization. Compression uses gain-shape sub-vector VQ (G=8, M=16, K=256 codebooks, 8-bit indices) with offline correlation-based channel reordering.

## Foundation

Roofline argument: decode attention has M=1, so it is memory-bound and extra rows cost almost no latency; cosine similarity decomposes into extra matmul rows sharing the same K^T read. Priority = beta*importance - (1-beta)*redundancy, with importance = mean attention over the last tau=8 queries and redundancy = softmax of row-summed key cosine similarity. Action choice maximizes a utility U<=0, i.e. the least information loss; all actions are irreversible.

## Compute

Modeled custom accelerator: 128x128 output-stationary PE array, FP16 multiply/FP32 accumulate, 64 MB on-chip SRAM (16 in / 16 weights / 32 out), HBM2 at 512 GB/s. RTL written in SystemVerilog and synthesized at 28nm, 500 MHz. Offline work: per-head k-means codebooks (K=256 per sub-vector) and channel permutations profiled on GSM8K; runtime needs top-k similarity tables, a running key accumulator and parallel DAU comparators. All speed numbers come from a cycle-accurate SCALEsim-v2 simulator, not a real chip.

## Reported

Simulation (SCALEsim-v2, 8192-token generation, budgets 1024-2048): up to 16.7x end-to-end throughput vs FP16 FullKV, 5.5x vs Oaken, 4.9x vs Ecco, 1.8x vs R-KV; QwQ-32B 50%-FP over 10x FP16 at 8192 tokens; max batch >10x FullKV at budget 256 and 20-40% over R-KV. Compression overhead: R-KV 23% of runtime at budget 2048 vs Gossamer under 2%; 1.25x over R-KV at budget 512 and 1.79x at 2048. VQ 48 bytes vs 256 bytes per FP16 vector = 5.3x; reordering error 0.118 to 0.035 (3.4x). Synthesis: 1.46 mm^2, 811 mW, 0.68% area and 0.65% power overhead. Accuracy: LongBench v2 DS-Llama-8B 30.4% vs FullKV 30.8% at budget 2048; QwQ-32B MATH-500 budget 256 55.0%, nearly 2x R-KV; ablations peak at beta=0.1, 50-75% FP allocation, action mix Evict 87.6% / Quantize 10.1% / Merge 2.3%. Paper also cites KV cache under 2% of memory at 512 tokens and over 84% at 128K, and a 512-entry global compression step costing up to 12x one attention computation.

## Limitations

Speedups are simulator-only on a modeled 28nm ASIC with HBM2, not measured on hardware. The free-lunch claim depends on attention being memory-bound and non-batchable across requests. Gains appear only after KV length exceeds the budget, so short contexts see no benefit. VQ needs offline profiling and per-head channel permutations, and beta, wmerge/wquant and the FP split need tuning. No code or seeds. It is a hardware architecture paper (cs.AR), not an agent or decoding-control method.
