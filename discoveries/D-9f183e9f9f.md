## Problem

Sub-3-bit post-training quantization of Qwen3.8-27B keeps short-task scores (AIME) but, in published comparisons, solves at most 72% as many DeepSWE tasks as BF16. The paper says low-bit methods are calibrated on short web text and scored by perplexity, while a DeepSWE task averages about 150 tool calls, 50 minutes, and a 92K-token median context.

## Technique

NariQ is trellis-coded PTQ. It keeps QTIP's bitshift trellis and a computed codebook (as in EXL3), random Hadamard rotations, and YAQA two-sided rounding, kept only when two-sided rounding cuts held-out curvature-weighted error by at least 5%. The loss is KL from BF16 only at tokens the BF16 model wrote; tool output stays in the prefix. Bit width per module comes from a gradient estimate of that module's own quadratic loss, rescaled by the trellis MSE ratio (about 4x per bit; they use ~3.9x from 3-bit to 2-bit), combined across width assignments, then a multiple-choice knapsack. Linear layers are floored at 2 bits. The 248,320-row output is split into 16 bands of about 15,500 rows ranked by mean predicted probability, each at least 2 bits. Scales and norms are tuned 70% on coding-agent traces and 30% on BF16 replies to general prompts. Released files are custom GGUF types TRL1-TRL6 (fork IDs 44-49), block size 16.

## Foundation

QTIP: on near-Gaussian rotated weights, a trellis at 2 bits has MSE 0.069 against a rate-distortion bound of 0.063. A second-order expansion of next-token KL around BF16, plus the claim that quantization errors at different widths are nearly uncorrelated (curvature-weighted cosines 0.035, 0.009, 0.004 between the 3-bit error and the 2-, 4-, and 5-bit errors on four probe matrices). Logit KL curvature is p(1-p), so likely tokens get more output bits.

## Compute

No GPU-hour figure for calibration, allocation, or scale tuning. Building the files needs the BF16 model (card: 53.8 GB; paper: 55.6 GB checkpoint including vision and MTP). Inference needs the nari-llama.cpp fork, not stock llama.cpp, Ollama, or LM Studio. NVIDIA compute capability 8.0+, Linux driver 570+ or Windows driver 572+, CUDA 12.8 release binaries. With the BF16 vision projector, a 24 GB GPU fits about 135K / 160K / 184K context at 3.0 / 2.5 / 2.0 bpw; full 262K context is about 30 / 28 / 27 GB. Kernels are tuned for SM120 (RTX PRO 6000). Tested NVIDIA GPUs: RTX PRO 6000, H100, L4, A10, plus one unqualified RTX 5060 Ti. No AMD or Intel kernels. Pre-Ampere NVIDIA falls back to CPU. Apple M3 Pro (18 GB), built from source: about 3.8 tok/s at 3.0 bpw and 4.9 at 2.0 bpw.

## Modifications

Nothing in this release runs inside DRock's Ollama path. TRL tensor IDs 44-49 are fork-specific. Intel Arc has no NariQ kernel, and the smallest language-model file is 6.75 GB before a BF16 KV cache, which already exceeds the 6 GB Arc A380. A useful port would need upstream or Ollama tensor support, an Intel or CPU kernel with a measured tok/s on the i5-8400, KV quantization (they list it as future work), and the unreleased quantizer retargeted at DRock's small Qwen. That is a new runtime, not a patch on the executor or the listed failing tests.

## Limitations

One model, Qwen3.8-27B. Their own 'level with BF16' claim covers 3.0 and 2.5 bpw only; 2.0 bpw is behind by 7.3 DeepSWE points and 11.7 AIME points in the paper. The model card's 3-round DeepSWE counts do not match the paper's one-attempt counts, so the headline pass@1 is not a single reproducible figure. Cross-harness bars versus Unsloth are explicitly not controlled. Vision stays BF16, MTP is omitted, KV and activations stay BF16. GeForce is one unqualified 5060 Ti run (~38 tok/s, 2.5 bpw, short prompt, ~47K context on 16 GB). CPU decode exists and is described as slow for 27B, with no i5-class number. About 13% of vocabulary tokens appear as generated calibration tokens; the rest are protected only by the 2-bit floor.
